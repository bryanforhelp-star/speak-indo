# Day 1 — AM — Foreign Learner — *terima kasih, kak*

**Archetype:** Foreign learner
**Length:** 25–30s
**Setting:** Cafe table in Bali, scooter helmet visible on the chair, iced
coffee in shot. Natural light.
**Caption (TikTok/Reels/Shorts):** if you're going to Bali, the first thing
you need to fix is how you say thank you 🇮🇩 #bali #bahasa #balitravel

---

## Hook (0–2s)
*(close to camera, slightly amused)*

> "If you're going to Bali, the first thing you should fix is how you say
> thank you."

**On-screen text:** *Going to Bali? Fix this first.*

## Teach (2–10s)
*(pulls back, holds up one finger)*

> "Most foreigners just say *terima kasih.* It works — but it sounds like
> a textbook. Locals add one tiny word."

**On-screen text:** *terima kasih = thank you*

## The phrase (10–18s)
*(says it slowly, then naturally)*

> "*Terima kasih, kak.* Kak just means *older sibling* — but everyone uses
> it. It makes you sound like you live here."

**On-screen text appears as it's spoken:** *terima kasih, kak* (then sub:
*thank you, friend*)

## Situation (18–25s)
*(b-roll: paying at a warung, smiling at the cashier, the cashier smiles
back)*

> "Try it next time you pay for coffee. The smile you get back is wild."

## CTA (25–30s)
*(back to camera)*

> "I'm practicing this stuff for 5 minutes a day at speak-indo.com — link in
> bio."

**On-screen text / end card:** *Practice free → speak-indo.com*

---

## Variations to test
- Swap "fix this first" for "learn this before you land"
- Try opening with the phrase instead of the hook ("*Terima kasih, kak* —
  this is why locals smile back")
