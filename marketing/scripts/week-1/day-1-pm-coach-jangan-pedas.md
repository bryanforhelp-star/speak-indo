# Day 1 — PM — Indonesian Coach — *jangan pedas*

**Archetype:** Indonesian / local coach
**Length:** 20–25s
**Setting:** Bright simple background or warung table with a plate of food in
front of the coach. Daylight.
**Caption:** if you can't handle Indonesian spice, save this 🌶️ #bahasa #bali
#indonesia

---

## Hook (0–2s)
*(direct to camera, warm smile)*

> "If you don't want spice in Indonesia, *no spicy* doesn't really work."

**On-screen text:** *"no spicy" ❌*

## Pivot (2–7s)

> "Most warungs will smile and nod — and still bring you spicy food. Here's
> what locals actually say."

## Teach (7–16s)
*(slow, clear, then natural)*

> "*Jangan pedas.* *Jangan* means *don't*. *Pedas* means *spicy*. So:
> *don't spicy.* Try it: *jangan pedas.*"

**On-screen text appears in steps:**
- *jangan = don't*
- *pedas = spicy*
- *jangan pedas = no spice*

## Situation (16–22s)
*(gestures to the food, mimics ordering)*

> "Use it when you order: *Saya mau nasi goreng, jangan pedas.* I want fried
> rice, no spice. Easy."

## CTA (22–25s)
*(small smile)*

> "Practice it free at speak-indo.com. Your stomach will thank you."

**End card:** *Practice free → speak-indo.com*

---

## Notes
- Pronunciation matters here — coach should hit *PEH-das*, not *PEE-das*.
- Optional: end with the coach saying *"selamat makan"* (enjoy the meal) and
  a quick subtitle.
